The brief02 / 10
Where we would start

Not a pop-up. Not a logo on a wall.

The house began in Paris in 1837, making harnesses for horses. Almost two centuries on, one artisan still makes a bag from start to finish, by hand.

So the room cannot be fast. It has to move at the speed of a hand.

Code oneThe horse
Code twoThe hand
Code threeTime
The idea03 / 10
Built on one stitch

The saddle stitch: two needles, one thread, pulled tight from both sides. Slower than a machine, and it outlasts one.

We would build the whole evening on that rhythm. In, and out. Breath, and heat. Two sides of one thread.

The Saddle Stitch
A 90-minute ritual · Three rooms · 24 guests an evening
Room one · 30 min04 / 10
The Salt Room

Breathe, in a room of salt.

Walls of rock salt, lit from behind. A floor of salt from the Rann of Kutch, raked by hand between sessions into the pattern of a printed scarf. Guided breathwork, lying down, in near silence.

The silk is not on display. It is folded under each guest’s head.
Room two · 25 min05 / 10
The Manège

Heat, in the round.

A manège is the round arena where a horse is schooled. So the heat room is round too: tiered cedar, no front row, everyone facing the centre.

Every eight minutes, steam is poured over vetiver, the Indian khus root, and the whole room breathes out together.

Room three · 35 min06 / 10
The Unhurried Hour

Rest, and nothing to buy.

A low, quiet room. Guests are wrapped in silk after the heat, handed cold tulsi and a slow cup of kahwa, and left alone. No counter, no catalogue, no one selling.

The product is in every room and sold in none of them.

The house already proved this abroad, with a gym where the scarves were the equipment.

Paris codes. Indian materials.

The benchesCedar

The steamVetiver

The horseMarwari

The curled-ear horse of Rajasthan, in the film.

A Paris house should feel at home here, not on a visit.
